03 AI Defence

Latest updates · 21 October 2026

In the full issue →

The UN's disarmament research chief warns that AI is crowding out human judgement in war, as the US Congress weighs human-oversight rules for AI weapons and autonomous-weapons talks run into verification problems.

Research, industry & policy

  • UNIDIR: AI on track to 'crowd out' human decision-making in warfare, UN News · 20 Oct 2026: Director Robin Geiss said AI now spans intelligence analysis, targeting, drones and cyber operations; UNIDIR's Centre of Excellence on AI, Peace and Security is launching the UN system's first multi-stakeholder forum on military AI, covering industry guidelines, an 'AI security lexicon' and misuse by non-State armed groups.
  • Congress considers safeguards as the military speeds up drones and AI, WHRO / WUNC · 20 Oct 2026: Senate Armed Services provisions in the annual defence bill would require human oversight of AI-controlled weapons, ban AI for nuclear launch or detonation and bar its use for domestic surveillance.
  • Autonomous weapons talks advance, but verification obstacles remain, Janes · 20 Oct 2026: A special report on discussions under the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons towards a possible legally binding instrument on lethal autonomous weapon systems, and why verifying compliance is hard.

Conferences, workshops & events

  • Future of Defense Command & AI Summit – US Edition, 3 Dec 2026 · Online: Free virtual summit on AI in command, wargaming and decision support (10:00 ET).
  • MORS Emerging Techniques Forum 2026, 8–10 Dec 2026 · Arlington, VA: Theme: AI for the operations research community.

Why it matters

Speed is the selling point of military AI and also its main risk; oversight rules and verification will decide whether humans stay in the loop.
