Latest updates · 20 October 2026

Australia's first national study of AI in online child sexual victimisation finds more than a third of 16–18-year-olds ask AI for life advice every week, and a Nature Mental Health paper says youth safety frameworks for generative AI must work in practice.

Research, industry & policy

  • Teens experiencing AI-assisted online sexual abuse, and seeking help from AI, Adelaide University · 19 Oct 2026: The Australian Cybercrime Observatory surveyed almost 2,000 people aged 16–18: 36.6% ask an AI for personal life advice each week and 22.2% for psychosocial counselling; at least 1 in 25 have experienced, or have a close friend who experienced, AI-involved online sexual victimisation before 18.

Journals & preprints

  • Improving safety frameworks for generative AI and youth well-being policy (Nature Mental Health), Nature Mental Health · 19 Oct 2026: A perspective by Siva Mathiyazhagan, Lucia Nazzaro and Desmond Patton on turning platform safety frameworks into youth-centred practice.
  • Vision Models Capture Complementary Representations in Children That Converge in Adults, arXiv 2610.16860 · 12 Oct 2026: How the relationship between image-text aligned and vision-only models changes across development remains unknown.

Conferences, workshops & events

  • Machine Learning: Intro to AI for kids (series starts), 9 Nov 2026 · Online (Zoom): Free afternoon sessions introducing machine learning to young learners.
  • Hour of AI: new activities launch, 16 Nov 2026 · Online: New Hour of AI activities for every grade, with free webinars and a toolkit.
  • Hour of Code: Generation AI Workshop, 21 Nov 2026 · El Paso, Texas (La Nube): Hands-on AI coding workshop for kids and families.

Why it matters

If teens turn to chatbots for advice and counselling every week, those systems need safe routes to real help, and policy has to measure what happens in practice.
