Latest updates · 20 October 2026

In the full issue →

The FDA's comment window on generative-AI medical devices closed on 19 October with the AMA and AHA both filing views, and the FDA cleared an MRI-derived AI tool to estimate breast-cancer recurrence risk.

Research, industry & policy

  • FDA clears TumorSight Risk for HR+, HER2– breast cancer, OncLive · 19 Oct 2026: The 510(k) covers an AI score estimating 5- and 10-year recurrence-free survival from routine breast MRI, pathology and clinical data in stage I–IIIA disease. In 2,138 patients the high-risk group had a hazard ratio of 2.81.
  • AMA sets out a path for FDA oversight of genAI, American Medical Association · 19 Oct 2026: The AMA backs a least-burdensome, risk-based approach that protects patients while supporting responsible device innovation.
  • AHA comments to FDA on genAI-enabled devices, American Hospital Association · 19 Oct 2026: Hospitals filed feedback on the FDA discussion paper (docket FDA-2026-N-7874), whose comment period closed on 19 October.

Journals & preprints

  • Complementary Vulnerabilities: Auditing and Governing Clinician-LLM Error Overlap, arXiv 2610.25279 · 18 Oct 2026: Health systems are deploying large language models (LLMs) into clinical workflows, often on the assumption that a second automated check can only reduce error.
  • Medical Fine-Tuning Reshapes, Not Reduces, Patient-Facing AI Risk: A Rubric-Based, LLM-Judge Evaluation Framework, arXiv 2610.24807 · 17 Oct 2026: Patient-facing medical question answering with large language models (LLMs) is moving from research demonstrations to production deployments, yet evaluation remains anchored on factual correctness.

Conferences, workshops & events

  • ACC webinar: AI in Clinical Practice – Cardiac MRI, 4 Nov 2026 · Online: Live webinar on AI applications in cardiac MRI, 1 pm EST.
  • Digital Heart Summit 2026, 6–7 Nov 2026 · Berlin (i-LAB): AI and digitalisation for cardiac risk management in AF and heart failure.
  • AHA Scientific Sessions 2026, 6–9 Nov 2026 · Chicago (McCormick Place): Late-breaking science on AI in CVD diagnosis; Health Innovation Pavilion 7–9 Nov.

Why it matters

With comments closed, the FDA now has to turn a discussion paper into a framework for devices built on foundation models, the next wave after narrow imaging AI. News summary only, not medical advice.
