Clinical AI shows real gains in care pathways: AI triage sped up pulmonary embolism care in a German hospital, Microsoft reports strong results on hard cases, and an AI stethoscope is cleared.

Research, industry & policy

  • Microsoft's MAI-DxO study published in Science, TechCrunch · 22 Oct 2026: In simulated work-ups of complex NEJM case records, the diagnostic orchestrator was right in 83% of cases against 36% for generalist physicians; specialists were not compared.Evidence: Peer-reviewed (evidence step 5 of 6)Key finding: On difficult published case records, MAI-DxO reached the correct diagnosis far more often than generalist doctors working under test conditions.Significance: Clinical: shows sequential, step-by-step AI diagnosis can be tested rigorously, but only in simulation so far.Takeaway: Promising for complex cases; prospective trials with real patients are still needed.New todayMicrosoft
  • FDA clears Eko's Core 300 AI stethoscope, Cardiovascular Business · 23 Oct 2026: The device combines analogue and digital listening, amplifies sound up to 35 times and is cleared for adults and children.Evidence: Company announcement (evidence step 1 of 6)New todayUS FDA
  • Experts on AI to close gaps in India's public healthcare, YourStory · 23 Oct 2026: A TechSparks panel with Niramai and Wadhwani AI discussed screening and decision support for overstretched public clinics.IndiaNew todayIndia and Andhra Pradesh

Journals & preprints

  • Real-world AI-assisted PERT workflow and its association with care processes and budget impact, European Heart Journal – Digital Health · 20 Oct 2026: In a before-and-after study of 424 patients with pulmonary embolism, AI flagging on CT raised response-team activation from 9.2% to 84.5% and halved the time to interventional therapy.Evidence: Peer-reviewed (evidence step 5 of 6)Study design: Retrospective before-and-after cohort, one university hospital; Sample size: 424 patients; Outcomes: PERT activation, time to diagnosis and therapy, costsKey finding: AI triage raised PERT activation from 9.2% to 84.5%, cut CT-to-diagnosis time from 2.1 to 1.3 hours and halved time to intervention.Significance: Clinical: links an AI tool to faster escalation in a time-critical cardiovascular emergency, with modelled cost savings.Takeaway: Single-centre and retrospective; length of stay and 30-day mortality fell but not significantly.New today

Why it matters

The strongest evidence is for AI that speeds up existing clinical pathways; diagnostic claims on test cases still need trials. Research summary only, not medical advice.
