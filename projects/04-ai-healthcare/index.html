Latest updates · 22 October 2026

In the full issue →

Two strong clinical studies lead today: a multicentre randomised trial found an AI navigation system made CT-guided lung biopsies safer, and an independent audit across 17 hospitals found a cleared pulmonary-embolism AI missed more clots than its benchmarks suggested.

Research, industry & policy

  • FDA clears Elucid's plaque-aware FFR-CT software, Cardiovascular Business · 21 Oct 2026: The software estimates coronary blood-flow limitation from CT using plaque make-up as well as anatomy; the company reports 92.2% sensitivity and 93.1% specificity in its ELUCIDATE study.Evidence: Company announcement (evidence step 1 of 6)New todayUS FDA
  • ECG plus blood-test AI flags heart-transplant rejection, Cardiovascular Business · 20 Oct 2026: NYU Langone researchers trained models on more than 5,300 ECGs from 389 adult recipients; a model adding two rejection blood tests did better than ECG alone in a 38-patient test group.Evidence: Peer-reviewed (evidence step 5 of 6)Sample size: 5,300+ ECGs from 389 recipients for training; 38 recipients in the test groupNew todayCardiology
  • Abu Dhabi publishes an AI Clinician Framework, HealthTech Asia · 21 Oct 2026: The Department of Health set rules for AI systems that carry out clinical tasks, unveiled at the Abu Dhabi Future Health Summit.New today

Journals & preprints

  • A Generative AI-powered navigation system for precision CBCT-guided lung biopsies: a randomised controlled trial, npj Digital Medicine · 21 Oct 2026: GPS-Lung cut needle punctures (2.0 vs 2.5), procedure time (20.4 vs 23.2 min), radiation dose and pneumothorax (12.7% vs 23.9%) against standard guidance.Evidence: Peer-reviewed (evidence step 5 of 6)Study design: Multicentre randomised controlled trial (NCT06762613); Outcomes: Punctures, procedure time, radiation dose, pneumothoraxKey finding: In a multicentre RCT, AI navigation lowered pneumothorax after lung biopsy from 23.9% to 12.7% and cut punctures, time and radiation.Significance: Clinical: a randomised trial, not a retrospective test, showing fewer complications in an everyday procedure.Takeaway: Strong evidence for this setting; wider replication and longer follow-up should come before routine use.New todayClinical trials
  • Multi-site real-world performance of commercial AI for pulmonary and incidental pulmonary embolism, npj Digital Medicine · 21 Oct 2026: Across 30,678 CT pulmonary angiograms and 37,191 other CT scans, sensitivity was 86.8% for PE and 73.5% for incidental PE, with specificity above 99%; the vendor had no role.Evidence: Peer-reviewed (evidence step 5 of 6)Study design: Multi-site retrospective real-world evaluation in a 17-facility academic health system; Sample size: 30,678 CTPA and 37,191 CT examinations; External validation: Yes: independent of the vendor; Outcomes: Sensitivity and specificity for PE and incidental PEKey finding: Real-world PE sensitivity was 86.8% (72.9% sub-segmental) and incidental PE 73.5%, below FDA-clearance benchmarks.Significance: Clinical: an independent, large check shows cleared imaging AI can underperform in routine practice.Takeaway: Hospitals should audit cleared AI locally and keep radiologist review, especially for small clots.New todayMedical imaging
  • The anatomy of a personal health agent, Nature · 21 Oct 2026: An open-source agent splits work between data-science, domain-expert and health-coach sub-agents, assessed on 11 benchmark tasks with more than 7,000 annotations and 1,350 hours of expert and user effort.Evidence: Peer-reviewed (evidence step 5 of 6)Key finding: A three-part personal health agent was evaluated on 11 tasks with 7,000+ annotations and 1,350 hours of expert and user time.Significance: Technical: a rare large, structured evaluation of a consumer health agent rather than a single demo.Takeaway: Useful design blueprint; it is not a clinical outcome trial.New todayOpen-source and open-weight AIBenchmarks
  • ECG for laboratory values:A comprehensive physiological and technical analysis, arXiv 2610.28507 · 20 Oct 2026: A review of how far the ECG can stand in for blood tests, covering both physiology and modelling.Evidence: Preprint (evidence step 4 of 6)New todayCardiology
  • Manifold-Informed Neural Networks for Low-Label ECG Electrolyte Inference, arXiv 2610.28322 · 20 Oct 2026: Uses the geometry of ECG data to estimate blood electrolytes when few labelled recordings exist.Evidence: Preprint (evidence step 4 of 6)New todayCardiology

Why it matters

Randomised and independent real-world evidence is what clinicians need; today's two npj papers show both the gains and the gaps of clinical AI. News summary only, not medical advice.
