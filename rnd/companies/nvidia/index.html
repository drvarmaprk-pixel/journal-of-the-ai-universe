Full write-up from 22 October 2026

1. Company and research division. NVIDIA: the Kumo structured-data team (Kumo Relational), NVIDIA Healthcare with the UW Medicine Institute for Protein Design (RFD4-Proteina), and NVIDIA Robotics (Isaac platform). All three were announced at GTC Berlin.

2. Date of publication / announcement. 21 October 2026. The Kumo Relational technical blog on Hugging Face is marked 'Published October 21, 2026'. The healthcare and Isaac press releases are dated 21 October 2026 (GTC Berlin). The underlying Kumo Relational paper is arXiv:2604.12596 (first posted April 2026; its newly released model and leaderboard results are what is new here).

3. Research title and field. (a) Kumo Relational: a pre-trained relational foundation model that predicts directly from multi-table databases (field: structured-data foundation models, relational deep learning). (b) RFD4-Proteina: an open protein-design model in the BioNeMo family (field: generative protein design). (c) Isaac platform expansion: Isaac Lab 3.0, the Isaac OS runtime and the Agile ONE SN reference humanoid (field: robot learning and embodied AI).

4. Research background. Most business prediction (churn, demand, fraud) runs on relational databases. The usual pipeline flattens many linked tables into a single row per entity by hand-built aggregates, then trains a separate gradient-boosted tree or neural model for each task. Earlier tabular foundation models generally work on one flat table. In protein design, the challenge is searching an enormous design space for sequences and structures that perform a specified function. In robotics, researchers often stitch together separate simulation, training and deployment tools, which slows the path from a learned skill to a physical robot.

5. Scientific innovation. Kumo Relational reads the database as it is: tables, primary-key/foreign-key links and timestamps. It adapts to a new task purely in context, using up to 10,000 labelled historical examples supplied at inference time, with no task-specific training or feature engineering. A small network first reasons within each table (alternating attention over columns and rows); a larger network then passes row embeddings across the key links with graph attention, interleaved with attention across the context examples. Because cross-table reasoning works on row embeddings rather than every cell, cost does not grow quadratically with the number of cells. Pre-training uses only synthetic relational databases from a generator built on PluRel (ICML 2026). RFD4-Proteina couples programmable protein generation with search, so scientists or software agents can steer designs with molecular inputs, constraints and scoring objectives. The Isaac update packages data, simulation, learning and on-robot runtime into one path.

6. Methodology. Kumo Relational was tested on 41 tasks drawn from 15 relational databases (RelBench V1 and V2, SAP SALT and 4DBInfer) spanning e-commerce, medical, enterprise, social and academic data, none of which were used in pre-training. Baselines were supervised relational deep-learning models, expert-engineered gradient-boosted tree pipelines, AutoGluon ensembles and recent tabular foundation models. Accuracy was also placed on the public RelArena-α leaderboard as Elo against median inference time per 1,000 samples. Predictions use the Predictive Query Language (PQL); temporal tasks replay past database states up to an anchor time so that no future data leak into the context. RFD4-Proteina was compared with unnamed baselines on four suites (protein binding, small-molecule binding, enzyme scaffolding and DNA binding) in a research draft that was not reviewed for this report. The Isaac items are platform releases, not experiments.

7. Results. Kumo Relational (company-reported):

  • RelBench V1 classification: about 4 AUROC points above gradient-boosted trees on expert-engineered features, on average.
  • SAP SALT: 7 MRR points above XGBoost out of the box and 13 points after fine-tuning; 8% better MRR than AutoGluon ensembles (16% after fine-tuning) and about 25% better than recent tabular foundation models.
  • RelArena-α: Elo 1,842 at about 17 s per 1,000 samples. The top entry (rt-plurel, Elo 1,878) takes about 7,300 s, roughly 430 times slower.
  • Robustness: works with only 0.2% of the training data as context, stays stable with up to 75% of foreign-key links removed and degrades little with many noisy columns added.
  • Scale: a custom graph engine handles databases of 500B+ rows by memory-mapped I/O at up to 5 GB/s.

RFD4-Proteina: higher computational design success rates and generation throughput than the evaluated baselines on all four suites; no figures were given in the release.

Agile ONE SN (specification, not a result): 1.40 m, 40 kg, 31 degrees of freedom, 3 kg payload per arm, two 20-degree-of-freedom hands with 1 kHz force control and tactile sensing, Jetson Thor onboard compute (2,070 FP4 teraflops, 128 GB unified memory). Isaac Lab 3.0 is generally available; the SimReady library offers more than 2,000 simulation-ready assets.

8. Scientific significance. If the Kumo results hold up independently, they would be the first strong case that a single few-shot foundation model can replace task-specific supervised pipelines on multi-table enterprise data, the setting where most commercial prediction actually happens. It also gives software agents a declarative way to ask predictive questions of a database. RFD4-Proteina puts an open, searchable protein-design model from a leading protein-design laboratory into wide circulation through cloud and AI partners. The Isaac release lowers the integration burden for humanoid research by offering a standard reference robot and an open learning framework.

9. Technical limitations.

  • All Kumo figures come from the developer. The arXiv paper is a preprint, and the leaderboard is young.
  • In-context prediction used at most 10,000 examples; NVIDIA says task-specific supervised models can still win when very large training sets exist, and predictive signal must lie within a limited number of table hops.
  • The model weights carry the OpenMDW licence v1.1 (code is Apache 2.0); users should check licence terms before commercial use.
  • RFD4-Proteina's success is computational. Laboratory experiments decide whether designs work, and no quantitative results were published in the release.
  • Agile ONE SN ships in early 2027; no task-performance data exist yet.

10. Technology readiness and evidence type. Kumo Relational: commercial (open-source library, NIM microservice, released weights); evidence type: company technical blog plus preprint (arXiv:2604.12596); marketing-grade comparisons, not independently reproduced. RFD4-Proteina: open model released; evidence: press release citing an unreviewed research draft (preliminary). Isaac Lab 3.0: commercial (generally available). Agile ONE SN: validated prototype, with availability planned for early 2027.

11. Competitive comparison. On relational prediction, the reported baselines are the right ones (XGBoost on engineered features, AutoGluon, relational deep-learning models and tabular foundation models), and the speed–accuracy trade-off against the top RelArena-α entry is large. Comparison with the Databricks and SAP stacks is indirect: SAP SALT is SAP's own enterprise benchmark, and a client notebook runs on Databricks. For physical AI, the same day saw AMD and Generative Bionics announce a real-to-sim-to-real data factory on AMD Helios infrastructure, a different route to the same robot-data bottleneck.

12. Future research (1–5 years) — FORECAST. FORECAST (1–5 years): independent re-evaluation of relational foundation models on RelBench-style suites is likely within a year; vendors may add relational in-context prediction to database and warehouse products; protein-design models such as RFD4-Proteina are likely to be wired into autonomous-laboratory loops with experimental feedback; reference humanoids may become shared benchmarks for whole-body manipulation.
